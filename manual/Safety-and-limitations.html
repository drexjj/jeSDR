jeSDR 1.5.0

Safety and limitations#

Safety#

  • SWR protection covers every transmit path. A trip holds the drive at 1 % and ignores remote drive changes until it is cleared (Transmit).
  • Transmit band plan: with a country plan set, the radio will not key outside it (Band plan).
  • An HPSDR client that stops sending during transmit is un-keyed after 1 s; a TCI client that disconnects while keyed is un-keyed at once.
  • A browser that keyed the radio is un-keyed if it disconnects or goes silent for 3 s.
  • Stopping jeSDR (More → Shut down → Stop jeSDR, or the service) un-keys and saves first; a crash drops the T/R relay and the external PTT before the process exits.
  • The speaker is held silent for the first 1.5 s after start-up.
  • The PA power calibration transmits only after the three checks and a second tap, stops on an SWR above 3:1, and holds every other way of keying or tuning off while it runs.
  • The transmit timeout un-keys every source after 10 minutes without a break; battery protection holds the power down on a low battery and stops transmitting on a flat one (Power & battery).

Always make the first transmission on each interface into a dummy load with a wattmeter: send a single tone, check it lands on the correct side of the carrier and that the power follows the drive.

Limitations#

  • The built-in digital modes are FT8, FT4, FT2, JTTY, RTTY, PSK and RADE V1. JS8 is not built in: run JS8Call on the Pi in DIGI mode. For anything else use WSJT-X or fldigi through Hamlib, or a TCI/HPSDR program.
  • RADE's end-of-over callsign is not sent or shown.
  • TCI carries IQ, not receive audio; programs on the Pi use the jeSDR Receive / jeSDR Transmit audio devices.
  • An HPSDR client is the master of the frequency: a retune from Hamlib or the console is not reported back to it (TCI clients are told).
  • HPSDR clients see one VFO; split and RIT are available over TCI, split also over Hamlib.
  • Only one program should transmit at a time.
  • Browser audio is 16 kHz mono with 80–300 ms of delay: fine for voice and digital-mode monitoring, too slow for CW sidetone (none is sent).
