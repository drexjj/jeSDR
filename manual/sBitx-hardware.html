sBitx hardware and upgrades#

Notes on the sBitx V3 itself: its specifications, microphones, and the Raspberry Pi 5 and Touch Display 2 upgrades. To get inside, see Opening the case.

sBitx V3 specifications#
Supply11.5–14 V DC; full power needs 12.8–14 V at up to 9 A
Receive currentabout 600 mA
Transmit currentup to 9 A at full drive
Bands80 to 10 m

Typical output per band:

Band80 m60 m40 m30 m20 m17 m15 m12 m10 m
Power25 W25 W25 W25 W22 W18 W15 W12 W10 W

jeSDR's PA power calibration sets the drive for each band against these figures. Use a supply that holds its voltage while transmitting: a sagging supply costs power on the high bands and can upset the Pi (see Power & battery).

Microphones#

The stock microphone has low output and a hollow sound. Mic gain, the compressor and the transmit equalizer help, but a better microphone helps more.

The mic jack#

The sBitx mic input is a 3.5 mm TRS jack:

ContactSignal
TipMic +
RingPTT (to ground to transmit)
SleeveGround
plug base  ===| GND |===| PTT |===| MIC+ |=>  plug tip

Few other radios wire a 3.5 mm plug this way, so another hand microphone usually needs its plug rewired or an adapter. Hand mics owners have used:

  • TIDRadio TD-HM060: replace its 2.5 mm TRRS plug with a 3.5 mm TRS plug.
  • Baofeng-style speaker-microphones: rewire to a 3.5 mm TRS plug as above, and disconnect the speaker inside.

A better element in the stock microphone#

The stock mic's electret capsule can be replaced, for example with a PUI Audio AOM-5024L-HD-R (−24 dB, noticeably more output and a clearer sound).

  1. Open the microphone. Photograph the inside first: note the capsule's wire polarity and how the PTT switch and its spring sit.
  2. Unsolder the old capsule, leaving the wires on the board.
  3. Solder the new capsule with the same polarity.
  4. Put a little loose cotton around the capsule to damp resonance.
  5. Close it up, checking the PTT spring is back in place.

Then lower Mic gain to suit, and check the ALC meter.

A USB headset or microphone#

A USB headset or USB sound card can be the radio's microphone, headphones or both: see Headset and USB audio.

Raspberry Pi 5#

jeSDR runs on a Pi 5 as well as a Pi 4. The swap needs more than a new board:

  • Power. The Pi 5 needs a 5 V regulator that can give 5 A; set its output to about 5.3 V to make up for the drop in the wiring. Too little and the Pi won't start or resets. vcgencmd pmic_read_adc shows the supply rails, and jeSDR's Pi badge turns red on under-voltage.
  • Display cable. The Pi 5's display connector is the small 22-pin type; the 7-inch display needs a Standard to Mini display cable (the Raspberry Pi 500 mm one fits the case).
  • Cooling. The Pi 5 runs hotter: fit the Raspberry Pi Active Cooler or another fan and heatsink. jeSDR shows the Pi's temperature and warns when it is hot; keep it under about 70 °C.
  • Case. The Ethernet and USB ports and the mounting holes are in different places, so the side panel needs new cut-outs (and perhaps new standoffs). 3D-printable replacement side plates are on the Discord server.
  • Software. Run the one-line installer once on the Pi 5, even after writing the SD card image (the image is made on a Pi 4).

Afterwards, check the display, the audio, receive and a low-power transmit into a dummy load, and look at More → Diagnostics and dmesg for errors.

Touch Display 2#

The Raspberry Pi Touch Display 2 (720 × 1280) is sharper than the original 7-inch display, but it is not a drop-in replacement:

  • It doesn't fit the old bezel. It is taller and narrower; a 3D-printable bezel is on the Discord server.
  • Power connector. It has a different power plug: cut and solder the power wires, black to ground, brown to +5 V. Check the polarity twice.
  • Display cable. The existing cable can be reused (on a Pi 4); insert it with the exposed contacts facing up.

Software:

  1. Bring Raspberry Pi OS up to date: sudo apt update && sudo apt full-upgrade.

  2. In /boot/firmware/config.txt, make sure the display driver line is the KMS one (dtoverlay=vc4-kms-v3d, not vc4-fkms-v3d), and add:

    # Touch Display 2
dtoverlay=vc4-kms-dsi-ili9881-7inch,rotation=270

    Restart.

  3. In the desktop's screen settings, turn DSI-1 to the orientation you want, and map the touchscreen (Goodix Capacitive Touchscreen) to it.

jeSDR's screen adapts to the new size. The battery saver dims the display through the kernel's backlight control, if the display provides one.
