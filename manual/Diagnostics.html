Diagnostics#

More → Diagnostics has five self-tests for the radio's hardware (PA health, receiver health, SWR bridge check, the LPF sweep, audio and I²C). The PA bias adjustment and the frequency calibration, which use the same machinery, are on the Calibration page. One runs at a time. Each result is kept on the radio (~/jeSDR/data/diag_<test>.json) and shown on the page the next time you open it. Rows are marked OK, Check or Fault, and the findings under each table say what to look at. Tests that transmit ask for a second tap and stop at once with Stop (or the Stop on the transmit strip). While a test runs nothing else can key, tune or change the mode; the SWR protection is held off during the transmitting tests, which stop by themselves on an SWR above 3:1.

The PA health, receiver health and SWR bridge checks compare with the previous run, so run them once while the radio works well to have a baseline.

PA health#

Needs a dummy load rated for the board's full power (25 or 40 W). About two minutes.

  1. With the radio receiving, then keyed with no drive, it measures the supply current. The difference is the PA's idle (bias) current.
  2. On each band (all but 60 m), it steps the drive 20, 30 … 100 % and measures the power, the supply current and voltage.

For each band it reports the power at full drive, the current, the efficiency (output power over the extra DC power the PA draws), the supply voltage under load and the 1 dB compression point: the drive where the power falls 1 dB short of what the low-drive steps predict, i.e. where the PA stops getting louder.

The compression point is measured from the PA's best gain between 20 and 60 % drive, so the lower gain at very low drive (the push-pull pair near its crossover) isn't mistaken for compression.

It points out: almost no idle current (bias off, an open MOSFET or supply), an idle current that has moved since the last check (drifting bias, failing MOSFET), a PA that limits at 60 % drive or less on 80–17 m, efficiency under 20 %, a supply that drops more than 1 V under load, a band well below the others on 80–17 m, and any band that has lost a quarter of its power since the last check. 15–10 m give less power and saturate sooner by design (the push-pull PA's losses rise with frequency), so they are judged only against their own last check.

Current, bias and efficiency need the INA260 power monitor; without one only the power is measured.

Receiver health#

Put a dummy load or a 50 Ω terminator on the antenna jack. It does not transmit; about 15 seconds.

On each band it captures the receiver's IQ and measures the noise floor (dBFS/Hz, the median from 1 to 18 kHz either side of the dial) and any narrow spurs 15 dB or more above it, with their frequency. Each band is shown against the median of the others. More than 8 dB less noise means the band has lost gain (its low-pass filter relay, a solder joint, the front end); more than 8 dB more noise, or a spur 20 dB or more up, is interference made inside the radio. A band that has moved more than 4 dB since the last check is marked too. Some slope across 80–10 m is normal. The IF gain stays as you set it.

SWR bridge check#

  1. Dummy load: with a 50 Ω dummy load on the jack, a 5 W carrier on each band. The reflected reading should stay near zero; an SWR above 1.3 means the bridge is out of balance there (or the load isn't 50 Ω at that frequency), so the SWR meter and protection read high.
  2. Open jack: take the dummy load off and tap Open jack. A 2 W carrier for a moment on each band. The reflected reading should be close to the forward one; under 70 % means the reflected side under-reads, so the SWR meter and protection would read low into a bad antenna.

The table shows both halves (the bridge's raw counts, 0–1023). The bridge's power calibration is BRIDGE (PA power and calibration).

LPF sweep (low-pass filters)#

The Low-pass filters section of More → Diagnostics (or search the settings for "lpf") measures the transmitter's four output low-pass filters and points out one that looks defective, damaged, built with a wrong or misplaced part, or not attenuating as it should.

FilterBands
LPF_A15, 12, 10 m
LPF_B20, 17 m
LPF_C40, 30 m
LPF_D80, 60 m

You need a 50 Ω dummy load rated for 25 W or more on the antenna jack (no antenna, tuner or amplifier).

  1. Carrier: 1–10 W; 5 W is plenty. The power reading can briefly go above it (the drive is corrected at each frequency, and the PA's gain changes from band to band), hence the 25 W dummy load.
  2. Filters: B, C and D are selected. LPF_A is always swept: it is the reference the others are measured against.
  3. Tick the three checks and tap Start sweep, then Tap again to transmit.

The radio keys a carrier and steps it from 1.5 to 36 MHz in 500 kHz steps, plus the centre of every band. Each filter is swept from just below its own bands upward (LPF_D from 3 MHz, C from 6, B from 12, A over the whole range): its passband and the harmonics it has to stop. At each frequency it holds each filter in circuit in turn and averages the SWR bridge's forward and reflected readings. The relays and the frequency only ever change with the carrier muted. A run takes three to five minutes (it times the radio's audio delay at the start and waits that long at each change), with a short rest in receive every 45 seconds. It transmits outside the amateur bands too, into the dummy load only.

How it measures. The bridge sits after the filters, so its reading is the PA's output times the filter's response. The PA's output alone changes by several dB across HF (LPF_A's own curve shows it), so a filter can't be judged from its reading by itself. LPF_A passes everything up to about 30 MHz, so it serves as a reference: each other filter's reading divided by LPF_A's at the same frequency and drive is that filter's own response, with the PA and the bridge cancelled. In a filter's own bands expect it within a dB or so of LPF_A, either way: the filter is a slightly different load on the PA. The bridge cannot read much more than about 35 dB down at 5 W; deeper parts of the stopband are reported as "at least" that (lpfsweep show all lists every frequency).

For each filter it reports:

  • Loss in its own bands against LPF_A (should be under 1.5 dB; over 2.5 dB is a fault). LPF_A can't be measured against itself: if it were lossy, the other filters would all read high in their bands, so the median of those readings is its figure.
  • −3 dB, where it rolls off above the top of its range, from its own level in its bands.
  • 2nd and 3rd harmonics of its lowest band, the hardest case: 12 dB or more at the 2nd and 20 dB or more at the 3rd is expected. Above 30 MHz LPF_A is rolling off too, so the figure there is a lower bound. LPF_A's own stopband is above the sweep's range: only its passband is checked.
  • SWR in its own bands (a mismatched filter shows a higher SWR than LPF_A at the same frequency).
  • Findings: dips inside the passband, a filter that passes almost nothing (relay not closing, an open connection), and what has changed since the previous sweep. Run it once on a radio that works well to have a baseline.

If the radio's SWR bridge turns out to be ahead of the filters, the forward reading hardly changes with the filter; the sweep notices and judges each filter instead by how strongly it reflects above its cutoff (a high SWR there is right).

The last result is kept on the radio (~/jeSDR/data/lpf_sweep.dat, and the one before it as lpf_sweep.dat.prev) and shown when you open the page. A stopped sweep shows what it measured but is not saved.

  • The SWR protection is held off during the sweep (a filter in its stopband may show a high SWR on purpose); the sweep keeps the power low instead. It stops on an SWR above 3.5 through LPF_A in its passband (check the dummy load), no output power, no bridge readings, or battery protection.
  • While it runs nothing else can key, tune or change the mode. Stop, or the Stop on the transmit strip, ends it at once.

Console: lpfsweep start [W] [A,B,C,D] (e.g. lpfsweep start 5 C,D), lpfsweep stop, lpfsweep status, lpfsweep show (the stored result as a table with the findings), lpfsweep show all (every frequency too).

Audio and I²C#

Does not transmit or tune; about 5 seconds.

  • Codec inputs: receive, mic and the audio from programs such as WSJT-X (jeSDR Transmit): level (RMS), peak, DC offset and clipped samples. Clipping on receive means lower the IF gain or use ATT; a DC offset over 2 % points at a coupling capacitor or the codec's input.
  • Audio drop-outs during the test and since start (speaker, capture, jeSDR Receive).
  • I²C: the Si5351 (0x60), the SWR bridge (0x08) and the INA260 (0x40) are each probed 50 times, with each device's failed transactions since start. A device that misses probes has an intermittent fault: wiring, pull-ups, a noisy supply, or RF getting into the bus.

Console#

diag pa [bands]              diag rx [bands]
diag freq [MHz] | apply      diag bridge load|open [W]
diag audio
diag show <test>             diag stop | status | help

Bands: e.g. 80m,40m,20m or all.
